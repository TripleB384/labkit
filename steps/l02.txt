LESSON 2: Text Tools & Regex   (Linux+ objective 1.5)
============================================================
Setup needed first: bash ~/labkit/u1.sh

GOAL: Dig through a fake app log and customer list like a real admin.

--- Step 1: Find every ERROR with its line number.
  $ cd ~/linuxlab
  $ grep -n ERROR app.log
  You should see:
    5:...ERROR db: connection refused
    9:...ERROR api: POST /invoice 500
    12:...ERROR disk: /var is 97% full

--- Step 2: Count the warnings.
  $ grep -c WARN app.log
  You should see:
    3

--- Step 3: Search without caring about upper/lower case.
  $ grep -i "DATABASE" app.log
  You should see:
    2 lines with 'connected to database'

--- Step 4: Show everything that is NOT INFO, and count it.
  $ grep -v INFO app.log
  $ grep -v INFO app.log | wc -l
  You should see:
    The WARN and ERROR lines, then: 6

--- Step 5: Use regex: lines that END with 'full'.
  $ grep 'full$' app.log
  You should see:
    3 disk lines (81%, 88%, 97%)

--- Step 6: Admin classic: show only real settings (no comments, no blank lines).
  $ grep -v '^#' config/app.conf | grep -v '^$'
  You should see:
    port=8080
    host=0.0.0.0
    db_host=localhost
    db_user=poolapp
    debug=true
    smtp_server=mail.example.com

--- Step 7: Cut out the name and city columns.
  $ cut -d, -f2,3 customers.csv | head -4
  You should see:
    name,city
    Ana Reyes,Springfield
    Ben Carter,Riverside
    Carla Diaz,Springfield

--- Step 8: Use awk: customers paying more than $100 (skip the header row).
  $ awk -F, 'NR>1 && $5 > 100 {print $2, $5}' customers.csv
  You should see:
    Ana Reyes 149
    Dev Patel 149
    Iris Chen 149
    Kim Nguyen 149

--- Step 9: Use awk to add up monthly revenue.
  $ awk -F, 'NR>1 {sum+=$5} END {print sum}' customers.csv
  You should see:
    1228

--- Step 10: Replace text with sed. Then prove the file did NOT change.
  $ sed 's/Basic/Starter/' customers.csv | head -3
  $ grep -c Basic customers.csv
  You should see:
    Ben Carter shows 'Starter'
    Then: 4  (file still says Basic)
  Why: sed only prints the change. Add -i to actually edit the file.

--- Step 11: See why uniq needs sort.
  $ uniq servers.txt
  $ sort servers.txt | uniq -c
  You should see:
    First: all 7 lines (nothing removed)
    Then:
      1 cache01
      2 db01
      1 web01
      3 web02

--- Step 12: Count customers per city, biggest first.
  $ cut -d, -f3 customers.csv | tail -n +2 | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
  You should see:
    5 Springfield
    3 Riverside
    2 Lakeside
    2 Fairview

--- Step 13: Find the busiest IP addresses in the web log.
  $ awk '{print $1}' access.log | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn | head -3
  You should see:
    4 10.0.0.5
    3 192.168.1.20
    2 10.0.0.7

--- Step 14: Page through a file with less. Search, then quit.
  $ less app.log
  You should see:
    Type /ERROR then Enter to jump to it. Press n for next. Press q to quit.

STUCK?
  * awk prints weird stuff: Use single quotes around the awk program: '...'. Double quotes let the shell eat $2.
  * uniq didn't remove duplicates: They weren't next to each other. Pipe through sort first.
  * Stuck inside less: Press q.
